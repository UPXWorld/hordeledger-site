Silk, Heavy Leather and Iron Bar Hit the Vendor Floor

A week after launch, three of the busiest mid-level materials on the Horde auction house have fallen almost all the way to what a vendor pays for them. Iron Bar sits at 2s 42c against a vendor price of 2s 00c, down from a 28s 32c daily median on November 5. Silk Cloth and Heavy Leather are both around 1s 90c against a 1s 50c vendor price. Prices this close to the vendor floor have little room left to fall, and that's what makes these materials worth a look today.

A 90% slide in eight days

Launch week priced everything as if it were rare. As more players reached the level 30–45 zones, supply caught up, and the fall has been steady rather than a single crash. Iron Bar went from 28s to 7s 40c to under 5s, and then to 2s 42c this morning. Silk Cloth dropped from 7s 78c to 1s 97c and has stayed flat around 1s 98c for three days. Heavy Leather is still slipping a few copper a day.

These are not dead markets. Sell-through estimates put Silk at up to about 11,500 units a day and Heavy Leather at up to about 8,200, with thousands listed at any time. Those figures count every unit that disappears between scans, cancellations included, so read them as an upper bound. Even so, buyers are taking these materials as fast as farmers list them.

How close is "close to vendor"?

Each item has a fixed price a vendor will pay for it. When the auction-house price gets near that number, sellers stop listing because the vendor pays almost as much without the wait. That puts a soft floor under the price. Five busy materials are now within about 30% of theirs:

The seller's view: the gap is small

For a farmer, these prices mean the AH barely beats the vendor. After the 5% auction cut, an undercut listing nets only a little more than vendoring:

Thirty copper a unit is still worth taking on a full stack, but it's no longer the launch-week bonus. If the price drops another 15–20%, vendoring becomes the better choice and listings should thin out on their own.

The buyer's view: limited downside

For crafters, this is about the cheapest these materials can stay for long. The most a buyer can lose to a further drop is roughly the gap to the vendor price, about 20–30%. Nothing here says prices will go back up. Demand could stay where it is for weeks. But a materials stockpile bought near the floor carries little price risk.

One small crafting angle: in Classic, a Steel Bar is smelted from one Iron Bar and one Coal. At 2s 42c plus 4s 55c, that's 6s 97c of materials against an 8s 01c lowest listing, which nets 7s 61c after the cut. The margin is about 64c, and Coal is thin with only 99 listed, so it's a modest edge, not a sure thing.

What to do

  • Crafters: if you'll level Tailoring, Leatherworking or Blacksmithing through the silk, heavy leather and iron tiers, now is a low-risk time to buy those materials.
  • Farmers: compare the AH price with the vendor price before you list. With only about 30c a unit extra, the vendor is a fine fallback if the price slips again.
  • Miners: Iron Ore is 2s 12c and Iron Bar 2s 42c, so smelting adds only about 30c a bar right now. Sell whichever saves you time.
  • Everyone: eight days of history is short. Check the live price before you commit gold.
