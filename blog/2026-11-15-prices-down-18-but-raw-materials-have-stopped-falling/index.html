Prices Fell 18% This Week, but Raw Materials Have Stopped

The Horde price index stands at 32.5 this morning, down 18.4% from a week ago and down two thirds since launch. But the headline hides a split. Trade goods as a group have been flat for three days, and the everyday basket we track has risen 4.5% from Thursday's low, to 11g 05s. What is still getting cheaper is the finished stuff: potions, recipes and gear.

The slide is running out of speed

The index measures the typical item against its own first-day price. It lost 20 points on November 6 alone. Over the last four days it has lost about one point a day: 35.4, 34.6, 33.6, 33.5, and 32.5 so far today.

The breadth numbers agree. Between November 8 and 9, 56% of items got cheaper and the typical item lost 7.6% in a day. Between November 13 and 14, 48% fell and the typical move was -1.3%.

Materials flat, finished goods still falling

Splitting the same calculation by group shows where the remaining fall comes from. These are my own cuts of the daily medians, so they won't match the economy tracker's category figures exactly; read the direction, not the decimals.

That is why the basket turned. It holds 21 things players actually buy in quantity (cloth, bars, leather, a few potions and herbs). It cost 16g 07s on November 9, bottomed at 10g 58s on November 13 and has risen two days running. Today's figure covers scans up to 07:00 only, so treat the last step as provisional.

Who is rising, who is not

Inside trade goods the picture is not uniform either. Low and mid-level materials are climbing, several of them while the number listed is growing too, which points to buyers rather than a shortage. Endgame goods that only appeared in volume last week are still finding their level.

Rugged Leather is the clearest case: the price is up and the shelf is thinner, with an estimated 5,900 a day leaving at most (that figure includes cancelled auctions). Runecloth is the opposite. Stock has tripled in four days and the price is still sliding toward its 4s vendor value.

The stability count for the whole house fits this. Of about 3,300 items with enough history, 1,082 are classed as falling and 171 as rising, but many of the risers are high-volume leveling materials, while half the list is still too jumpy to call.

How sure is this

Twelve days of history, and the first two were launch days with few items listed. Every item in the second table has hundreds to thousands of units listed, except Black Lotus at 81. Three flat days for trade goods is a pause, not proof of a floor; another wave of players reaching 60 could push endgame materials down again.

What to do

  • Leveling a profession on low-level materials: the "it'll be cheaper tomorrow" habit has stopped working for copper, light leather and starter herbs. Buy what you need when you need it.
  • Farmers: mid-level leather and mithril are paying more this week than last. Check the item page's listed count before a long session.
  • Buying endgame consumables, recipes or gear: these groups are still falling. If it can wait a few days, the data favours waiting.
  • Holding Runecloth: supply is growing faster than anything else on this list. The vendor floor is 4s; the market is at 6s 81c.
