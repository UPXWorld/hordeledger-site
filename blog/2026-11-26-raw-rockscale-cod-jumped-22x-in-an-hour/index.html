Raw Rockscale Cod Jumped 22x in an Hour. The Shelf Was Worth 1g

At 06:00 this morning the cheapest Raw Rockscale Cod on the Horde auction house was 4c, and it has stayed between 4c and 8c for two weeks. At 07:00 it was 88c, about 22 times as much. The surprise is how little stood in the way: the 2,688 fish listed an hour earlier were worth roughly 1g 08s all together.

Four floors that vanished overnight

Cod was not alone. Four of the biggest movers in this morning's data share one shape: a steady, cheap price, then a single scan where the cheap listings are gone and the next ask is 15 to 25 times higher.

"Old shelf" is the number listed at the last scan before the jump times the going price. It is a rough floor, since the dearer listings on a shelf cost more than the cheapest. Scans show asking prices only, so they cannot say whether those listings were bought or cancelled. Either way the result is the same: the cheap rungs are gone and a much higher ask is now the bottom.

The cod is the odd one. Stock went up, from 2,688 to 4,142, while the price rose 22-fold. So this is not a shortage: there are more fish for sale than an hour earlier, all of them at 88c or above. The belt shows the same thing over five hours, from 3 listed at 19g 50s at 02:00 to 21 listed at 13g 44s by 07:00.

How often this happens, and how long it lasts

We went back through every hourly scan since November 12 and counted each time an item with at least 10 listed jumped to five times its typical price of the previous 24 hours. That happened 1,768 times, around 130 a day. This is routine, not a freak event.

The typical old shelf in those 1,768 cases was worth about 2g 06s. That is the real finding: for most cheap goods on this realm, the whole bottom of the market is worth a couple of gold. A 20x move in a 4c fish says very little about what the fish is worth.

Iridescent Pearl shows why. Its typical daily price has sat between 8s 26c and 11s 46c for two weeks, and single scans have printed 70s, 74s and now 1g 81s on the market estimate. Each earlier print was gone by the next day.

How sure is this

The base rates rest on 15 days of hourly scans and are solid. This morning's four jumps are not: cod and pearl are one scan old, the stew and the belt five hours. The stew (28 listed) and belt (21 listed) are small markets where a couple of sellers set the price. The estimated 9,900 cod leaving the house per day includes cancelled listings, so it is an upper bound.

What to do

  • Buyers: if a cheap staple is suddenly 10x or 20x, check the item page's 14-day chart and wait. Six times in seven the old price was back within a day.
  • Sellers holding the item: a jump is a short window. List now, a little under the new floor; four in ten are over within six hours.
  • Anyone tempted to chase it: buying at the new price means betting against that 86%. The stock on the cod is higher than before the jump, not lower.
  • Reading movers lists: a huge 7-day change on an item that costs copper is usually one scan, not a trend. Look at the listed count and the day's typical price first.
