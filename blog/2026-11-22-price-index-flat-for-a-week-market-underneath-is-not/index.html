The Price Index Went Flat This Week. The Market Under It Did Not

For the first time since launch, the Horde price index ended a week where it began: 34.2 on November 15, 34.2 this morning. But only about one item in five is within 5% of last Sunday's price. Leveling materials rose 20 to 45%, several endgame materials lost more than half, and the two moves cancel out in the average.

A flat line after two weeks of falling

The index measures the typical item against its own first-day price. It fell from 100 to 50 in four days, and lost another 18% in the week to November 15. This week it stayed between 34.2 and 35.5.

The everyday basket (21 things players buy in quantity: cloth, bars, leather, a few potions and herbs) says the same. It costs 10g 72s now against 10g 82s a week ago, and has stayed between 10g 58s and 11g 00s since November 11.

Flat is not the same as calm. In my own cut of about 6,600 items with prices on both Sundays, half fell more than 2%, 37% rose more than 2%, and the typical item lost 2%. The stability count moved too: 380 items are now classed as rising against 171 a week ago, and 756 as falling against 1,082. About half the list is still too jumpy to call.

Going up: the leveling tier

Low and mid-level materials had the strongest week. These are deep markets, so the prices are not one seller's ask.

Heavy Leather is the one to notice. Eleven days ago it sat on its 1s 50c vendor value; it is now almost double that, with less than a third of the stock. Wool rose while its shelf grew, which points to buyers rather than a shortage: up to about 14,800 a day leave the house (an upper bound, since it includes cancelled auctions).

Globe of Water is now 59c above its 4s vendor value, and Runecloth is 1s 13c above its own 4s. Endgame is not all one way, though. Greater Eternal Essence rose from 1g 98s to 3g 25s (+64%) with 317 listed, and plain Thorium Bar barely moved at 20s 98c.

How sure is this

Nineteen days of history, and the first two were launch days with few items listed. Today's figures cover scans up to 07:00 only, a quiet hour, so they may shift by tonight. One flat week is a pause, not a floor. The number of units on the shelf also grew by roughly half this week, to about 2.4 million, and that much stock can push prices down again if buying slows.

What to do

  • Leveling a profession: tin, wool and low-level leather are no longer getting cheaper by waiting. Buy what you need when you need it.
  • Farmers on lower-level characters: Heavy Leather and Tin Ore pay well above their vendor values again, with thin shelves. Check the listed count on the item page before a long session.
  • Buying newer endgame materials: most of the second table is still falling. If it can wait a few days, the data favours waiting.
  • Holding Globe of Water or Runecloth: both are close to their vendor values. There is little left to lose by selling, and little to gain by listing below the vendor price after the 5% cut.
