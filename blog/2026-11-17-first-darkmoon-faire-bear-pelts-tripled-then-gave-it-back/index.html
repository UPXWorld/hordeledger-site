First Darkmoon Faire: Bear Pelts Tripled, Then Gave It All Back

The first live Darkmoon Faire ran November 9 to 16, and the auction house felt it in exactly three items. Torn Bear Pelt went from 1s 22c the day before the Faire to 4s 45c on day two, then slid back to 1s 20c by this morning. Small Furry Paws and Vibrant Plumes did a smaller version of the same thing. The crafted turn-ins did not react at all.

The beast drops: up fast, peak early, round trip

These are each day's typical market price (the median of that day's scans), from the day before the Faire to the day after it closed.

Two things stand out. First, the peak came on day two or three, not at the end. By Friday the 13th the pelt was already back to 2s 17c with three days of Faire left. Second, the round trip is almost exact: pelts, paws and plumes all sit within 2c of where they started. That happened in a week when the wider market fell, so holding flat is a touch better than it looks.

Soft Bushy Tail is the odd one out. It was still a brand-new market on November 7, with asks above 90s, and spent the Faire week finishing its launch slide. We can't separate a Faire effect from that, so don't read much into its row.

The crafted turn-ins: nothing

In Classic the Faire also takes crafted goods from blacksmiths, engineers and leatherworkers. If buyers had chased those, prices should have lifted during the week. They drifted down instead, in line with the rest of the market.

The one crafted item that climbed all week, Copper Modulator (6s 49c to 8s 50c), kept climbing after the Faire closed, so the Faire is a poor explanation for it.

Why pelts led

At Classic's turn-in rates, five pelts buy four tickets, which made pelts by far the cheapest ticket on the house before the Faire: about 1s 53c each. Buyers found that quickly. At the peak, a ticket cost about 5s 56c through pelts and 5s 45c through plumes (five for twelve tickets), near enough the same. That looks like buyers bidding each drop up until the cost per ticket evened out. We have not verified Forever's turn-in rates, so treat the per-ticket sums as Classic arithmetic.

How sure is this

These four markets are deep, with 300 to 1,200 listed on most days, so the direction is reliable. But it is one Faire, 14 days of history, and asking prices rather than sales. This Faire was in Elwynn Forest, a hard trip for Horde on a PvP realm, which may have held Horde demand down. The next one on our calendar opens December 7 in Mulgore; whether a home-turf Faire moves prices more is something only that week can show.

What to do

  • Farming bears, wolves or birds while levelling: keep the pelts, paws and plumes. This time they paid most on days two and three of the Faire, not before it and not at the end.
  • Buying turn-ins for tickets: prices are back at pre-Faire levels now. Last week, waiting until Friday cost half as much as buying on Tuesday.
  • Crafters: the data shows no Faire premium on grinding stones, bracers or leather armor. Don't craft them for the Faire on the strength of this one.
  • Everyone: one event is one data point. Check the item page during the next Faire before acting on this pattern.
