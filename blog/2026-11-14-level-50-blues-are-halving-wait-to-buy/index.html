Level-50 Blues Are Halving: Wait to Buy, List Fast

Rare and epic gear for level 48 and up is the fastest-falling corner of the Horde auction house. Of 123 such items with a price on both days, 80% are cheaper today than on November 12, and the typical one has lost 38%. Supply explains it: 174 pieces were listed late on November 11, and 716 at 07:00 this morning. If you're shopping for pre-raid gear, patience is paying several gold a day. If you're selling it, the opposite holds.

What the scans show

This market barely existed a week ago. On November 9 a dozen of these items had a listing at all. By November 12 it was 130 items, mostly with one to five copies each, priced by whoever got there first. Since then the shelf has filled and the early asks have been undercut again and again.

Among the 37 items with at least 8 copies listed now, 30 are down since November 12 and the middle one has halved. The fall hasn't slowed yet: Cord of Elements was 24g at 02:00 today and 14g 99s an hour later.

Why the "cheap" ones aren't flips

A simple screen flags these items as bargains, because today's lowest price sits far below the item's usual price. Lifestone's cheapest listing is 18g against a "usual" 60g. But that usual price is the middle of four days in which one to five sellers set the ask. It was never a level buyers were proven to pay.

The flow numbers point the same way. For Lifestone, roughly 9 copies a day have been arriving against at most 2.9 a day leaving, and "leaving" includes cancelled auctions, so real sales are lower. Apart from Devilsaur Gauntlets, the gear in the table sells an estimated 3 to 7 copies a day at best. With 10 to 20 listed and new copies arriving faster than that, the cheapest listing keeps getting undercut. Buying to relist means standing in that queue.

Where it might stop

The data can't say where the bottom is, and seven of the 37 well-stocked items rose over the same days, Shadowcraft Belt among them (25g 00s to 38g 55s). Two things are worth watching. First, items with a deep market early have fallen least: Devilsaur Gauntlets had 21 listed on November 12 and has the steadiest sales of the group, up to about 20 a day. Second, a few pieces are nearing their vendor value, which is a hard floor. Satyr's Touch asked a median 79g 20s on November 11 and 4g 00s this morning; a vendor pays 3g 46s for it.

Confidence: the direction is clear across more than a hundred items, but each single item has only three to four days of history and 8 to 24 copies listed. Individual prices can jump on one listing.

What to do

  • Buying to wear: unless the upgrade matters tonight, wait. Check the item page's listed count; while it keeps growing, the price has tended to keep falling.
  • Selling a fresh drop: list it now at the lowest price, on a short auction. Holding out for last week's ask cost the typical seller about 20% over the last day.
  • Flipping: skip level-50 blues for now. A low price against a four-day "usual" is not a discount when stock is up fourfold.
  • Bargain hunters: pieces listed close to vendor value carry little downside. That's the one spot where buying early is cheap to get wrong.
